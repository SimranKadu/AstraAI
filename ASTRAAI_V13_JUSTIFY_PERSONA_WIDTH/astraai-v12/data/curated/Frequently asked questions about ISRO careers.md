# Frequently asked questions about ISRO careers

Questions are written the way people ask them. The answers come from the ISRO Career Guide in data/careers. Add your own questions as new "##" headings, then run npm run train.

## Is there one exam for all ISRO jobs?

There is no one common ISRO entrance exam for all jobs. ISRO's Centralised Recruitment Board (ICRB) and individual centres advertise different positions and may use different selection methods. Always read the official notice for the exact post.

## Can a diploma holder join ISRO?

Yes. Technical Assistant posts commonly require a relevant engineering diploma that matches the vacancy. Recruitment for Technical Assistants is usually centre-specific and may use a written or computer-based test, a trade or skill test, or another assessment. Check the official notice for the exact discipline, marks and age limit.

## Can I join ISRO after Class 12 or 10th?

After 10th or SSLC, the usual route in the guide is a trade qualification: Technician-B posts commonly require SSLC or 10th plus the specified ITI trade certificate. After Class 12, the guide's route for engineering roles is Class 11 and 12 Science with PCM followed by a B.E. or B.Tech, and for science roles a B.Sc. followed by an M.Sc. Scientist and Engineer posts need the degree named in the notice.

## Which degree is needed to become a scientist or engineer at ISRO?

For Scientist/Engineer posts the guide lists B.E. or B.Tech in the discipline of the vacancy, such as Mechanical or Aerospace, Electronics and Communication, or Computer Science and IT. Space science research usually follows a B.Sc. then an M.Sc. in Physics, Mathematics or Astronomy, with a Ph.D. for many research roles.

## Is GATE compulsory for ISRO?

Do not assume GATE is compulsory for every Scientist/Engineer post, and do not assume GATE alone guarantees selection. Some recruitment drives shortlist by GATE score and some use their own written test, so the official advertisement decides.

## Where do I apply for ISRO jobs?

Apply only through the official ISRO Careers page and the ISRO Current Opportunities page. Beware of fake appointment letters and of agents asking for money, because ISRO does not require candidates to pay agents for appointment.

## How do I start preparing for ISRO exams?

Choose one target post and read its latest official advertisement. Confirm your degree, marks and age match the eligibility. Download the syllabus and previous papers, build strong fundamentals in your degree subjects, take timed mock tests and keep an error notebook. The guide suggests about one to two hours of core subjects, 30 minutes of problem-solving and 15 minutes reviewing mistakes each day, and a six-month plan.

## Which books should I read for ISRO exams?

The guide names no specific books. It advises using the books prescribed by your degree programme and standard references for your branch, and letting the official syllabus and past papers decide what to prioritise. Avoid buying large sets of books before you know your target exam.
